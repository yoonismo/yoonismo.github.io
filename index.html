Job market paper

Job market paperarXiv:2512.07176

Variational Regularized Bilevel Estimation for Exponential Random Graph Models

The exponential random graph model (ERGM) is a popular statistical network model for estimating the structural parameters of strategic network formation in economics and finance. Existing methods often produce unreliable estimates of parameters for the triangle, a key network structure that captures the tendency of two persons with shared friends to connect; such estimates may lead to untrustworthy policy recommendations for networks with triangles. Through a variational mean-field approach, my algorithm addresses the two well-known difficulties in estimating the ERGM, the intractability of its normalizing constant and model degeneracy. I introduce L2 regularization that ensures a unique solution to the mean-field approximation problem, and provide a non-asymptotic convergence rate analysis under mild regularity conditions. In Monte Carlo simulations the method achieves a 100% sign recovery rate for triangle parameters in small and mid-sized networks under perturbed initialization, compared with 50% for existing algorithms. A sensitivity analysis of the ERGM parameter estimates to hyperparameter choices offers practical guidance for implementation.

Work in progress

Wasserstein Distributionally Robust Estimation in Linear Quantile Regression with Missing Data

with Yanqin Fan and Gaoqian Xu

Estimators built on the missing-at-random assumption perform poorly once the missingness mechanism departs from MAR, while fully assumption-free approaches yield bounds too conservative to be useful. We propose a distributionally robust linear quantile estimator that uses the Wasserstein distance to measure departures from the MAR distribution: the estimator solves a minimax problem, maximizing the worst-case expected loss over a Wasserstein ball and minimizing it with respect to the parameter of interest.
